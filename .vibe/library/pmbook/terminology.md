# PMBOK Terminology

This document provides a **glossary of key terms** from the **Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK® Guide)**, the **standard for project management** developed by the Project Management Institute (PMI).

---

## Project Management Terminology

### **A**
- **Accept**: The act of formally recognizing that the deliverables of a project or phase meet the specified requirements.
- **Activity**: A distinct, scheduled portion of work performed during the course of a project.
- **Agile**: A project management approach characterized by **iterative and incremental delivery**, **flexibility**, and **collaboration**.
- **Assumption**: A factor in the planning process that is considered to be **true, real, or certain** without proof or demonstration.

### **B**
- **Baseline**: The approved version of a **work product** (e.g., schedule, cost, scope) that can be changed only through formal change control procedures.
- **Budget**: The approved estimate for the project or any work breakdown structure (WBS) component.
- **Business Case**: A documented economic feasibility study used to establish the **validity of the benefits** of a selected component lacking sufficient definition.

### **C**
- **Change Request**: A formal proposal to modify any document, deliverable, or baseline.
- **Chart**: A graphical representation of data (e.g., **Gantt Chart**, **Pareto Chart**).
- **Close Project or Phase**: The process of finalizing all activities across all Project Management Process Groups to formally complete the project or phase.
- **Constraint**: A limiting factor that affects the execution of a project (e.g., **scope, time, cost**).
- **Contingency**: A provision in the project management plan to **mitigate risks** (e.g., **contingency reserve**).
- **Cost Management Plan**: A component of the project management plan that describes how costs will be planned, structured, and controlled.

### **D**
- **Deliverable**: Any unique and verifiable product, result, or capability to perform a service that is required to be produced to complete a process, phase, or project.
- **Dependency**: A logical relationship between two activities or between an activity and a milestone.
  - **Mandatory Dependency**: A relationship that is **contractually required** or inherent in the nature of the work.
  - **Discretionary Dependency**: A relationship that is **established based on best practices** or project team preference.
  - **External Dependency**: A relationship between a project activity and a factor **outside the project** (e.g., a vendor deliverable).
  - **Internal Dependency**: A relationship between project activities.
- **Duration**: The total number of work periods required to complete an activity or group of activities.

### **E**
- **Earned Value Management (EVM)**: A methodology that combines **scope, schedule, and cost** measurements to assess project performance and progress.
- **Estimate**: An assessment of the likely quantitative result (e.g., **cost, duration, resources**).
- **Execute**: The process of performing the work defined in the project management plan to achieve the project’s objectives.

### **F**
- **Float (Slack)**: The amount of time that a schedule activity can be delayed or extended from its early start date without delaying the project finish date.
  - **Total Float**: The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the **project finish date**.
  - **Free Float**: The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the **early start date** of its successor.

### **G**
- **Gantt Chart**: A **bar chart** that illustrates a project schedule, including **start and finish dates** of project elements.

### **H**
- **Historical Information**: Data and lessons learned from previous projects that can be used to **improve future projects**.

### **I**
- **Issue**: A current condition or situation that may **impact the project** (e.g., a risk that has occurred).
- **Iteration**: A **repetitive cycle** of work, often used in **Agile methodologies** (e.g., **Sprint** in Scrum).

### **K**
- **Key Performance Indicator (KPI)**: A **quantifiable measure** used to evaluate the success of a project or organization.

### **L**
- **Lessons Learned**: The knowledge gained during a project that shows **how events were addressed** or should be addressed in the future.

### **M**
- **Milestone**: A **significant point or event** in a project, program, or portfolio.
- **Monitor**: The process of **collecting, measuring, and disseminating** performance information to assess progress.

### **O**
- **Objective**: A **specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART)** goal for a project or phase.

### **P**
- **Phase**: A **distinct segment** of a project, typically marked by a **deliverable or milestone**.
- **Plan**: A **document** that describes how, when, and by whom a specific target or set of targets is to be achieved.
- **Portfolio**: A **collection of projects or programs** grouped together to facilitate effective management.
- **Program**: A **group of related projects** managed in a coordinated way to obtain benefits and control not available from managing them individually.
- **Project**: A **temporary endeavor** undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.
- **Project Charter**: A **document** issued by the project initiator or sponsor that formally authorizes the existence of a project.
- **Project Management**: The **application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques** to project activities to meet project requirements.
- **Project Management Plan**: The **document** that describes how the project will be executed, monitored, and controlled.
- **Project Manager**: The **person** assigned by the performing organization to lead the team responsible for achieving the project objectives.

### **Q**
- **Quality**: The **degree to which a set of inherent characteristics** fulfills requirements.
- **Quality Assurance (QA)**: The **processes and activities** performed to ensure that the project will satisfy the quality standards.
- **Quality Control (QC)**: The **process of monitoring and recording** results of executing the quality activities to assess performance.

### **R**
- **Resource**: A **person, material, or financial asset** required to complete a project.
- **Risk**: An **uncertain event or condition** that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on a project’s objectives.
  - **Risk Appetite**: The **degree of uncertainty** an entity is willing to accept in anticipation of a reward.
  - **Risk Tolerance**: The **degree, amount, or volume of risk** that an organization or individual will withstand.
- **Risk Management**: The **process of identifying, analyzing, and responding** to project risks.

### **S**
- **Schedule**: A **timeline** for completing project activities, including **start and finish dates**.
- **Scope**: The **work performed** to deliver a product, service, or result with the specified features and functions.
- **Scope Creep**: The **uncontrolled changes** or continuous growth in a project’s scope.
- **Stakeholder**: A **person or organization** that is actively involved in the project or whose interests may be positively or negatively affected by the project.
- **Stakeholder Analysis**: A **technique** used to identify and assess the influence of stakeholders on a project.

### **T**
- **Task**: A **small, discrete unit of work** required to complete a project.
- **Team**: A **group of individuals** working together to achieve a common goal.
- **Template**: A **predefined format** for a document or plan (e.g., **Project Charter Template**).

### **V**
- **Validation**: The **process of evaluating** a product, service, or result to ensure it meets the specified requirements.
- **Verification**: The **process of evaluating** whether or not a product, service, or result complies with a regulation, requirement, or imposed condition.

### **W**
- **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)**: A **hierarchical decomposition** of the project into smaller, more manageable components.
- **Work Package**: A **deliverable or project work** component at the lowest level of the WBS.

## Project Management Process Groups
The **PMBOK® Guide** organizes project management into **10 Knowledge Areas** and **5 Process Groups**:

### **Process Groups**
1. **Initiating**: Define a new project or a new phase of an existing project.
2. **Planning**: Establish the scope of the project, refine the objectives, and define the course of action.
3. **Executing**: Complete the work defined in the project management plan.
4. **Monitoring and Controlling**: Track, review, and regulate the progress and performance of the project.
5. **Closing**: Finalize all activities to formally close the project or phase.

---

## Knowledge Areas
1. **Project Integration Management**: Coordinates all project components.
2. **Project Scope Management**: Ensures the project includes all the work required, and only the work required.
3. **Project Schedule Management**: Manages the project timeline.
4. **Project Cost Management**: Manages the project budget.
5. **Project Quality Management**: Ensures the project meets the defined quality standards.
6. **Project Resource Management**: Manages the project team and physical resources.
7. **Project Communications Management**: Ensures timely and appropriate generation, collection, distribution, storage, and disposition of project information.
8. **Project Risk Management**: Identifies, analyzes, and responds to project risks.
9. **Project Procurement Management**: Manages purchases and acquisitions from external sources.
10. **Project Stakeholder Management**: Identifies and engages stakeholders to meet their needs and expectations.
