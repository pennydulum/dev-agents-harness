# Scrum Guide

This document summarizes the **Scrum Guide**, the definitive guide to **Scrum** as defined by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland. Scrum is a **lightweight framework** for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products.

---

## Scrum Overview
Scrum is founded on **empirical process control theory**, or empiricism, which asserts that knowledge comes from experience and making decisions based on what is observed. Scrum employs an **iterative, incremental approach** to optimize predictability and control risk.

### **Scrum Pillars**
1. **Transparency**: Significant aspects of the process must be visible to those responsible for the outcome.
2. **Inspection**: Scrum users must frequently inspect Scrum artifacts and progress toward a Sprint Goal to detect undesirable variances.
3. **Adaptation**: If an inspector determines that one or more aspects of a process deviate outside acceptable limits, and that the resulting product will be unacceptable, the process or the material being processed must be adjusted.

## Scrum Values
Scrum Team members learn and explore the values as they work with the Scrum roles, events, and artifacts:
- **Commitment**: Team members personally commit to achieving the goals of the Scrum Team.
- **Courage**: Team members have the courage to do the right thing and work on tough problems.
- **Focus**: Team members focus on the work of the Sprint and the goals of the Scrum Team.
- **Openness**: Team members and stakeholders agree to be open about all the work and the challenges with performing the work.
- **Respect**: Team members respect each other to be capable, independent people.

## Scrum Team
The Scrum Team consists of:
1. **Product Owner**
2. **Scrum Master**
3. **Developers**

### **Product Owner**
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Developing and explicitly communicating the **Product Goal**.
  - Creating and clearly communicating **Product Backlog items**.
  - Ordering **Product Backlog items**.
  - Ensuring that the **Product Backlog** is transparent, visible, and understood.

### **Scrum Master**
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide.
  - Ensuring the Scrum Team adheres to Scrum theory, practices, and rules.
  - Removing impediments to the Scrum Team’s progress.
  - Ensuring that all Scrum events are **productive and kept within the timebox**.

### **Developers**
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Creating a plan for the Sprint, the **Sprint Backlog**.
  - Turning Product Backlog items into **value** through work.
  - Adapting their plan each day toward the Sprint Goal.
  - Holding each other accountable as professionals.

## Scrum Artifacts
Scrum’s artifacts represent **work or value** to provide transparency and opportunities for inspection and adaptation.

### **Product Backlog**
- An **ordered list** of everything that is known to be needed in the product.
- **Attributes**:
  - **Description**: What is needed.
  - **Order**: The order in which items should be addressed.
  - **Estimate**: The effort required to complete the item.
  - **Value**: The business value of the item.

### **Sprint Backlog**
- A **plan** by and for the Developers for a Sprint.
- **Attributes**:
  - **Sprint Goal**: The objective for the Sprint.
  - **Selected Product Backlog items**: The items chosen for the Sprint.
  - **Actionable items**: The work needed to achieve the Sprint Goal.

### **Increment**
- A **concrete stepping stone** toward the Product Goal.
- Must be **usable** and meet the **Definition of Done**.

## Scrum Events
Scrum prescribes **five events** (also called ceremonies) to create regularity and minimize the need for meetings not defined in Scrum:

### **The Sprint**
- **Purpose**: A **container event** that encapsulates all other events.
- **Duration**: **1 month or less** (typically 2-4 weeks).
- **Goal**: Achieve the **Sprint Goal**.

### **Sprint Planning**
- **Purpose**: Initiate the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed.
- **Duration**: **Maximum 8 hours** for a 1-month Sprint (shorter for shorter Sprints).
- **Attendees**: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers.
- **Outcomes**:
  - **Sprint Goal**: The objective for the Sprint.
  - **Sprint Backlog**: The selected Product Backlog items and the plan to achieve the Sprint Goal.

### **Daily Scrum**
- **Purpose**: Inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as needed.
- **Duration**: **15 minutes or less**.
- **Attendees**: Developers (optional: Scrum Master, Product Owner).
- **Structure**:
  - What did I do yesterday to help the Scrum Team meet the Sprint Goal?
  - What will I do today to help the Scrum Team meet the Sprint Goal?
  - Do I see any impediments that prevent me or the Scrum Team from meeting the Sprint Goal?

### **Sprint Review**
- **Purpose**: Inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations.
- **Duration**: **Maximum 4 hours** for a 1-month Sprint (shorter for shorter Sprints).
- **Attendees**: Scrum Team and stakeholders.
- **Outcomes**:
  - **Feedback**: Stakeholders provide feedback on the Increment.
  - **Adaptation**: The Product Backlog is updated based on feedback.

### **Sprint Retrospective**
- **Purpose**: Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.
- **Duration**: **Maximum 3 hours** for a 1-month Sprint (shorter for shorter Sprints).
- **Attendees**: Scrum Team.
- **Outcomes**:
  - **Improvements**: Identify and plan improvements for the next Sprint.

---

## Scrum Rules
1. **Sprint Goal**: The Sprint Goal is the **single objective** for the Sprint.
2. **No Changes During Sprint**: No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal.
3. **Quality**: The Definition of Done ensures that all work meets the required quality standards.
4. **Transparency**: All Scrum artifacts must be **transparent** to ensure shared understanding.
